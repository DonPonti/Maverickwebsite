When people talk about startups in Telangana, Hyderabad usually gets all the attention. That makes sense because Hyderabad has a much larger technology ecosystem. But I think it is worth looking at what is happening closer to home in Warangal too.

Warangal has a large student population, universities, local businesses and an increasing number of people working in technology and digital services. That combination can create opportunities even if the city does not look like Hyderabad yet.

## What kind of businesses make sense here?

Not every business needs a huge office or a large investment. Digital marketing, software services, education, local commerce, design, accounting, food businesses and niche online services can all start relatively small.

The advantage for a local founder is that you can understand problems in the city first and build from there.

## Coworking is becoming more visible

There are now coworking and managed-office options in the Warangal-Hanamkonda area, which is useful for freelancers and small teams that don't want to rent a full office immediately.

That may sound like a small thing, but having a professional place to meet clients or work with a team makes a difference.

## Students are a big part of the opportunity

Warangal has a strong education base. That means there is a steady supply of students interested in software, design, engineering, finance and other skills.

The challenge is converting that talent into businesses and products instead of only using the city as a place to study before moving to Hyderabad.

## What could Warangal become known for?

I would not try to copy Hyderabad. A smaller city can have its own strengths: lower operating costs, local knowledge, education, tourism, agriculture-linked businesses and services for the surrounding districts.

Recent startup datasets also show that Warangal already has a meaningful base of registered startups, although registration alone does not tell us whether a company is active or successful. That distinction matters when looking at the ecosystem.

### The interesting part

The next few years will be worth watching. The question is not whether Warangal will suddenly become another Hyderabad. It is whether local founders can build companies that solve problems specific to Warangal and the wider region.

That is a much more interesting story to follow.
